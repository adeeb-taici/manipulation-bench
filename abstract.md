
Benchmarks for manipulative behavior in language models, where a model deceives or strategically misleads a counterparty to gain an advantage, typically vary one prompt axis in one environment. We test whether that suffices. We pre-registered an analysis plan and ran 13,590 scenarios across six multi-agent environments with six frontier models, varying three axes per environment: prompt framing, performance incentive (split into verbal incentives stated in the system prompt and structural incentives that change the payoff), and task difficulty. Across the six models, rank correlations of manipulation rates between environments average ρ = 0.055, with one pair (sales vs. debate) reaching ρ = −0.77. A model's manipulation rate in one environment is therefore a poor predictor of its rate in another, and aggregate manipulativeness rankings are unstable. The axis that drives behavior also depends on the environment type. In decision environments, where the model commits to a single action such as a stated valuation or a triage choice, framing and structural incentives account for most of the variance. In claim-generation environments, where the model produces claims that can be evaluated against a ground truth, as in sales pitches or candidate ratings, task difficulty dominates. We identified this split on five environments and confirmed it on a held-out sixth. Verbal performance incentives, of the form "you are graded on accuracy" or "user satisfaction is your primary metric," had no measurable effect in four of the six environments, and produced an effect in the remaining two only when paired with a concrete payoff change. Single-environment or single-axis benchmarks therefore risk missing the two largest sources of variation we identify, environment type and the verbal-versus-structural distinction within incentives, and verbal incentives without a payoff change appear unlikely to shape model behavior.
